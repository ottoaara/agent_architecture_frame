Vendor: Element (legal entity New Vector Ltd, UK), enterprise secure messaging built on the open Matrix protocol. Public information gathered October 2026; figures marked "third-party estimate" are unverified.

Company and services: Founded 2017 as New Vector by Matthew Hodgson and Amandine Le Pape, creators of the Matrix open standard; rebranded Element in 2020. Offers end-to-end encrypted, federated messaging, calls and collaboration via Element Cloud or the self-hosted Element Server Suite. Pricing published around USD 3 per monthly active user (baseline, on-prem from 250 seats) and USD 4 per MAU (Enterprise on-prem, includes data retention policies, mobile security pack and 99.9% uptime SLA).

Security: The platform and the Matrix standard are end-to-end encrypted by default (Olm/Megolm). The reference Rust implementation, vodozemac, was independently audited by Least Authority in 2022; the audit found implementation-level cryptographic issues that were addressed. Academic formal analysis of the Matrix cryptographic suite was published in 2024. Open source; federation means customers can run fully isolated, sovereign deployments.

Financial stability: About USD 48M raised in total, including a USD 30M Series B in July 2021 (Protocol Labs, Metaplanet, Automattic, Notion Capital). Revenue about USD 16.4M for the year to June 2025 (third-party estimate from filings). Element laid off core staff in 2022-2023 because it could not keep funding Matrix development alone, and in November 2023 relicensed its Synapse server from Apache 2.0 to AGPLv3 to sell commercial licences, a move the Matrix Foundation did not support. Recent growth is driven by government "digital sovereignty" demand.

Regulatory compliance: Element and all subsidiaries hold ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification (issued by Prescient Security). Government customers include the French government (Tchap, 300,000+ users), NATO, the German Bundeswehr and US DoD units (Navy, Marines, Space Force); listed on the UK G-Cloud framework. Self-hosting supports GDPR and data-residency requirements.
